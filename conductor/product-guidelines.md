# Product Guidelines: Sample Orchestrator

## Tone & Voice
- **Technical & Professional:** Use clear, precise language regarding audio engineering and music production.
- **Supportive & Instructional:** Provide helpful guidance and feedback to the user throughout the automated process.
- **Minimalist:** Focus on clarity and ease of use, avoiding unnecessary jargon where simple terms suffice.

## User Experience (UX) Principles
- **Efficiency-First:** Design interfaces that minimize the number of clicks required to move from recording import to final export.
- **Progress Transparency:** Clearly communicate the status of long-running audio processing tasks.
- **Error Forgiveness:** Provide clear, actionable feedback when automated analysis fails or when manual intervention is required.
- **Contextual Help:** Offer inline documentation and tooltips for complex DSP parameters and metadata fields.

## Visual Design
- **High-Contrast Interface:** Ensure legibility in various lighting conditions, common in studio environments.
- **Clear Information Hierarchy:** Use layout and typography to guide the user's attention to the most critical tasks and data.
- **Audio Visualization:** Provide clear and responsive waveform displays to help users verify and edit slice points.

## Content Standards
- **Precise Audio Terminology:** Use standardized terms like LUFS, BPM, SR/BD, etc., consistently across the application.
- **Descriptive Labels:** Every button, field, and menu item should clearly communicate its purpose and the result of interacting with it.
- **Informative Status Messages:** Avoid generic "Processing..." labels; use detailed messages like "Detecting onsets in Recording 01..." or "Classifying Drum One-shots...".
